AI Receptionist for General Contractors

A homeowner planning a $75,000 kitchen renovation calls during your lunch break, gets voicemail, and books with the next contractor before you call back. An AI receptionist answers every call 24/7, qualifies the project, and books the site visit before that happens.

The missed-call cost problem for general contractors

General contractors operate at the highest average job value of any residential trade. A kitchen remodel runs $30,000 to $120,000. A bathroom addition runs $20,000 to $60,000. A whole-home renovation can reach $300,000 or more. Each missed estimate call is not a $200 service fee you lost. It is a project that will appear in someone else's portfolio.

Homeowners do not leave voicemails and wait. They are calling three or four contractors at the same time, and the first one to schedule an in-person estimate often gets the job. When your phone rolls to voicemail, you are not just missing the call. You are handing the project to whoever picks up next.

The math

If a GC with a $60,000 average project misses one estimate call per week and closes 30% of estimates, that is one missed close every three to four weeks, or roughly $780,000 in annual revenue leak from voicemail alone.

How an AI receptionist works for a general contractor

1

Answers every inbound call in your company's name

Whether it is 7 AM on a Saturday or midday when you are on a job site, the AI greets callers as your business and takes the conversation from there.

2

Qualifies the project before booking the site visit

The AI asks the questions your estimator needs: project type, location, scope, timeline, and rough budget range. Callers with realistic projects get booked. Callers outside your service area or project range are handled politely without burning your estimator's time.

3

Books the site visit or discovery call directly to your calendar

No callback loop. The caller leaves the call with a confirmed date and time, reducing the chance they schedule with another contractor while waiting to hear from you.

4

Texts missed callers back in 60 seconds

If a call drops before booking, the AI texts the caller within about 60 seconds. Most homeowners are still on their phone. A 60-second text-back has a much higher response rate than a callback two hours later.

5

Reactivates past homeowners ready for the next project

A homeowner whose bathroom you finished two years ago is statistically likely to be planning their kitchen renovation now. The AI reaches out by name, references the prior project, and books the consultation before they start their Google search.

Renovation season surge

Every GC knows the pattern: January and February are quiet, then March hits and the phone rings constantly. Homeowners who spent winter researching and getting bids all converge on the same six-week window. If your office cannot handle the volume, you lose the projects you would have closed and you lose them permanently, because the homeowners book someone else and are off the market for another two years.

The AI handles unlimited concurrent calls. During your busiest intake window, every caller gets the same professional greeting and qualification flow, no hold music, no missed calls, no temp receptionist to hire and train.

Seasonal note

The spring surge is also the highest-quality window. Homeowners calling in March and April have typically done their research, have real budgets, and want to start by May. These are not tire-kickers. Every one you miss to voicemail is a funded project going to a competitor.

Evening and weekend calls

Most homeowners call about renovation projects in the evening or on weekends, the hours they have available after work when they are not worrying about something else. This is exactly when most GC offices are closed and calls go to voicemail.

The AI answers at 9 PM on a Tuesday the same way it answers at 10 AM on a Wednesday. A homeowner inspired by a renovation show on a Sunday evening can book a site visit before Monday morning. Your estimator shows up Monday with a booked calendar instead of a voicemail inbox to clear.

Past homeowner reactivation

The average homeowner undertakes a significant renovation every four to seven years. If you completed a deck or bathroom project for someone three years ago, they are likely approaching the window for their next project. They trust you, the sale is halfway done, and the average second project is comparable in size or larger than the first.

Belken includes past-customer reactivation in the AI Receptionist. You provide a list of completed homeowners with project dates. The AI reaches out by name, references the specific prior project, and books a consultation with anyone who is ready to discuss what is next. No cold outreach budget, no new lead cost, pure margin.
