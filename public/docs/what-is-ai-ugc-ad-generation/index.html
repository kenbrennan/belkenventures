The short answer

AI UGC ad generation is software that reads a script you write, puts it in the mouth of a realistic AI avatar, adds captions, and exports a vertical video ready for TikTok, Reels, or Shorts. The phrase UGC — user-generated content — refers to the style: a single person talking directly to a phone camera, conversational and unpolished, the way real creators post. AI tools replicate that format without a real person in front of a camera.

The workflow in Belken Studio: paste your script, pick an avatar, hit one button. Studio renders the talking head, burns the captions in, and delivers a vertical video sized for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts.

Why UGC ads perform

Traditional ads look like ads. UGC looks like a person talking about a product they actually use. That format outperforms polished production on direct-response campaigns for most social platforms. The result is lower ad costs, more scroll-stops, and higher conversion when the hook lands.

The catch has always been creator supply. Finding someone who fits the product, briefing them, waiting a week, and paying per video adds up fast. AI UGC tools collapse that to minutes per video at a fraction of the cost.

How AI avatar ad generation works

1

Write your script

Exactly what you want the avatar to say. Most short-form UGC ads run 20–60 seconds — that is 60–180 words. Hook in the first three seconds, product or proof in the middle, call to action at the end.

2

Pick an avatar

In Belken Studio, one avatar stays locked so every video in a campaign looks like the same creator. To update a line you edit the script and run it again — the face, voice, and style stay consistent.

3

Render

The software synthesizes a talking head that says your script word for word, with lip sync and natural head movement. Captions are generated and burned in automatically.

4

Post or download

Belken Studio lets you download the finished video or connect a YouTube or TikTok account and post straight from the app. Nothing is posted without you connecting the account and confirming the post.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between AI UGC and AI avatar tools?

The terms are often used interchangeably. AI avatar tools (HeyGen, Synthesia) focus on creating a realistic talking head from a script — used for explainers, training videos, and ads. AI UGC tools emphasize the short-form vertical format designed to look like organic creator content on TikTok or Reels. Belken Studio is both: it renders an AI avatar and outputs a vertical, captioned video in the UGC format.

Can I use AI-generated UGC ads on TikTok and Instagram?

Yes. TikTok and Meta both allow AI-generated video ads, subject to their standard advertising policies. The video must follow platform format specs: vertical 9:16, under 60 seconds for most TikTok placements, captions recommended. Belken Studio outputs a vertical video with captions already burned in.

Do I need to disclose that an ad uses an AI avatar?

Disclosure rules vary by platform and region and change frequently. FTC guidance in the US currently requires disclosure when the use of AI or synthetic media could mislead consumers about the nature of the content. Check the current policies of any platform you run ads on before posting. This is not legal advice.

What makes a good AI UGC ad script?

Short-form UGC ads work when the hook stops the scroll in under three seconds, the body delivers one specific claim or proof, and the close is a single clear action. Avoid corporate language — write the way a person talks. Read the script out loud before you paste it; if it sounds like a press release, rewrite it. The avatar delivers whatever you write, so the script is the entire creative lever you have.

How is Belken Studio different from HeyGen or Synthesia?
