What it is

Every service business has a dead list: past customers who hired them once or several times and then went quiet. They are not unhappy customers. They just moved on, forgot to schedule their next service, or got busy. They are also your most convertible leads because they already know and trust your work.

Dead customer reactivation is an outbound follow-up process that contacts those lapsed customers, by phone call or text, and gives them a clear reason to rebook. The goal is to pull revenue out of a list the business already owns, without spending a dollar on advertising.

The term "dead list" comes from CRM and email marketing terminology: contacts who have gone dark, stopped responding, or have not transacted in 90, 180, or 365 days. In the trades, the list lives in the job management software or a spreadsheet. Most businesses never touch it.

How it works

STEP 1

Pull the customer list

The starting point is any list of past customers: names, phone numbers, and the last date of service. This comes from job management software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro), a CRM, or a spreadsheet. Belken works with whatever format the business has.

STEP 2

Identify lapsed contacts

Customers who have not booked in 6, 12, or 24 months are the reactivation targets. The threshold depends on the service type: an HVAC maintenance customer should ideally book twice a year; a one-time job customer might be 18 months out. Belken segments the list and writes the outreach for each tier.

STEP 3

AI makes outbound calls and sends texts

The AI calls each contact in the business's name with a short, natural-sounding message written for the specific trade. It is not a robocall. The script identifies the business, references the past relationship, and offers a clear reason to rebook now, seasonal maintenance, a limited-time check-up, or a reminder that their last service was over a year ago.

STEP 4

Warm leads book directly to the calendar

When a contact expresses interest, the AI books the appointment to the business calendar. No hand-off required, no callback to schedule. The business sees new appointments arriving from customers who were already paid and gone. Urgent calls or customers who want to speak with a person are transferred live to the owner's cell.

Who needs it most

Any service business with a repeat-service model and a history of past customers who have gone quiet. The industries where reactivation works best are:

  • HVAC: Annual tune-up and maintenance customers are a predictable reactivation target. If a customer had a spring tune-up last year and has not called this spring, they are a reactivation candidate. The outreach message writes itself.
  • Plumbing: Past customers with recurring issues (old pipes, water heater age, slow drains) are worth a follow-up call offering an inspection. Customers who had an emergency plumbing job done are also candidates, they now know your number works.
  • Pest control: Quarterly service customers who lapsed after one or two treatments are the clearest reactivation targets. The pest problem did not go away; they just stopped calling.
  • Roofing: Post-storm follow-up with customers from past storm seasons. A homeowner who had a roof repair done two years ago during a hail season will get another hail season eventually.
  • Landscaping: Annual or seasonal contract customers who did not renew. A reminder call before the new season is a natural reactivation moment.
  • Electrical: Customers who had panel work or EV charger installs and may now need permits closed, inspections scheduled, or additional work as their needs grow.

Common questions

What is dead customer reactivation?

Dead customer reactivation is an outbound follow-up process that contacts past customers who have stopped booking jobs with your business. These are people who used your service once or several times and then went quiet, not because they had a bad experience, but because life moved on and they forgot to call back. Reactivation reaches out to them by phone or text to remind them you exist, offer a reason to rebook, and convert them back into paying customers.

Why is reactivating old customers cheaper than finding new ones?

A past customer already knows your business, already trusted you enough to hire you once, and does not need to be convinced you are credible. The cost to re-engage them is a phone call or text. The cost to acquire a new customer in the trades runs from Google Ads spend to leads from directories, referral programs, and marketing. Past customers convert at a higher rate than cold leads and require no ad budget. For most service businesses, the dead list sitting in their CRM or job management software is their cheapest source of new revenue.

What businesses benefit most from dead customer reactivation?

Any service business with a repeat-service model: HVAC (annual tune-ups, seasonal maintenance), plumbing (annual inspections, recurring issues), roofing (post-storm follow-up, annual gutter cleaning), pest control (quarterly service), landscaping (seasonal contracts), and electrical (panel upgrades, EV charger installs). If customers need the service more than once over their lifetime, reactivation works.

How does AI-operated dead customer reactivation work?

An AI-operated reactivation system works from the business's existing customer list. It calls or texts past customers with a scripted message written for the specific trade, identifies who is interested in rebooking, and hands off confirmed appointments to the business calendar. The business owner does not make the calls. The AI handles the outreach volume and routes warm leads to the team.

How many customers are typically in a dead list?

Most service businesses running for two or more years have hundreds of past customers who have not called in over 12 months. The exact number depends on how long the business has been operating and how many jobs they run per year. Even a small HVAC or plumbing operation typically has 200 to 500 lapsed customers in their job history. Reactivating even a small fraction of those translates directly to booked revenue.

Is dead customer reactivation the same as a marketing campaign?

It is different from advertising. A marketing campaign reaches cold prospects who have never heard of you. Reactivation reaches warm contacts who already know your work. The messaging, conversion rate, and economics are different. Reactivation is not about brand awareness, it is about reminding a known customer that the service they used before is still available and offering a clear reason to book now.
