What it does for your shop
Auto repair shops field the same five calls all day — estimate requests, appointment bookings, status checks, parts questions, and after-hours callouts. The AI handles every one of them without putting anyone on hold.

Answers every call in your shop's name, around the clock

The AI picks up in your shop's name — not a generic voicemail — and handles the call the way your service desk would. Callers get a real conversation, not a recording, whether it's 2 PM on a Tuesday or 8 PM on a Friday when everyone has gone home.

Handles estimate inquiries and books service appointments

When a customer calls for a brake estimate or wants to schedule an oil change, the AI collects the vehicle year, make, model, what they need, and their preferred drop-off time — then books the appointment directly to your calendar. They hang up with a confirmed time, not a callback promise that may or may not happen.

Texts missed callers back in about 60 seconds

When someone calls and can't get through — your service line is busy, a tech picked up and put them on hold, or the shop is closed — the AI texts them back within about a minute. The text opens a conversation that can move directly to a scheduled appointment. Auto repair customers who don't get an answer usually call the next shop on the list within minutes.

Calls dormant customers to rebook them

You have a list of customers who had their oil changed once and never came back. The AI calls each one by name, references their vehicle and when they were last in, and offers to get them scheduled for their overdue oil change or seasonal tire swap. Every customer you reactivate is recurring revenue you already paid to acquire once.

Transfers urgent calls to an on-call number you designate

For callers describing a safety situation — a brake failure on the highway, a vehicle that won't start — the AI can transfer to an on-call number you set up. Everything else gets handled conversationally, without waking anyone up at 11 PM for a next-week oil change request.

Why auto repair shops miss more calls than they think
The busiest shops are the ones most likely to miss inbound calls — because the service desk is already on the phone, under the hood, or dealing with a customer at the counter.

A missed call at an auto repair shop typically means one of three things: the caller gets your voicemail, the caller calls the next shop on Google Maps, or the caller calls back tomorrow and talks to a different shop. None of those outcomes puts money in your bay.

The same problem applies after hours. A driver who hits a pothole at 7 PM on a Friday and needs new tires before the weekend will call every shop on the first page of Google until someone picks up or responds. The first shop to answer that call — even with a text-back — almost always gets the job.

Dormant customer reactivation is the underused lever. Most auto repair shops have hundreds of one-time customers in their management system. Those people chose your shop once, had a good enough experience to pay, and then never came back — often not because of a bad experience, just because no one called them. An AI that systematically works that list for you turns sunk acquisition cost into recurring service revenue.
